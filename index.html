A Random Number Picker vs. Doing It the Old-Fashioned Way

Long before anyone typed "random number generator" into a search bar, people still needed unbiased picks. Here's how those old-school methods stack up against a modern number picker wheel.

Physical dice

Great for a 1–6 range, but limited beyond that, easy to lose, and impossible to use over a video call. A random number picker covers the same job, any range past six, and works the same way on every device in the house.

Slips of paper in a hat

Fair in theory, but slow to prepare, easy to shuffle unevenly, and awkward to repeat once the slips get soggy or torn. A number picker wheel gives the same "drawn from a pool" feeling in one click, with nothing left to refill or reshuffle between rounds.

Spreadsheet RAND() formulas

Technically random, but the formula recalculates unpredictably every time the sheet updates, which makes results hard to trust or reproduce, and most people never check whether the underlying formula is actually evenly distributed. A dedicated random number generator keeps each pick isolated and intentional.

None of these older methods are wrong, exactly — they just weren't built for how often modern life actually needs an unbiased pick. A browser-based random number picker keeps the fairness of a hat full of names or a fresh die roll, minus the setup time and the risk of losing a piece on the floor.
